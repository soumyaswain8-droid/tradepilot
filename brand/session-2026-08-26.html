The restart path — the bug that cost 2h40m

Tuesday's watchdog detected the dead floor correctly and restarted it twice. Both relaunches were dead within ten minutes having written nothing, not even an error.

The cause: the watchdog is itself a scheduled job, and macOS reaps the job's whole process group when the script exits — so a background child cannot outlive its parent. The watchdog logged "relaunched"; the floor was already gone. It now restarts the floor's own scheduled job, and verifies twelve seconds later that a process actually exists.

The chart read

12:13 — looking at the charts falsified the rule

I had the system draw the actual charts behind two of its own live signals and read them the way a person would. Both were no-trades, for the same structural reason.

StockWhat the chart showedWhy the rule was wrong
UDSCoiled in a narrow range all session, crossing the average price repeatedly A "dip and recovery" through a line price has crossed twenty times is not a trap being sprung
QUESSA textbook decline — each peak lower than the last: 374, 372, 370, 368 The rule would have bought into a steady fall, because a bounce back to the average satisfies the arithmetic

An average can never be a place where orders rest

Half of any day is below that day's average price — that is what "average" means. Price crosses it constantly, in every stock. So "dipped below the average and came back" is like saying "the temperature fell below today's average and then rose". Of course it did.

Nobody parks a standing order at an average. They park them at yesterday's low, today's low, at round numbers. Those can be swept. An average cannot.

Quantified before changing anything: signals on the average were 33% of Tuesday's and 42% of Wednesday's. Entries are now restricted to real levels where orders genuinely rest — cutting the largest single slice of candidates, the slice the pictures showed was worthless.

This is exactly what the chart-reading lane was for

The rule can only check arithmetic. Looking at the picture catches the case where the arithmetic is technically true and completely meaningless. It took two charts.

The stale-levels bug

14:15 — every agent was watching a price frozen at 9:16am

That "day high" row prompted a check. Each agent's levels are computed once, when it is assigned a stock. An agent that keeps its stock all session never refreshes them.

So "today's high" actually means the high of the first minute of trading, held for six hours.

StockLevel at 9:16Actual level laterDrift

Why this probably explains the loss

A stale level is not a level anyone is watching. The entire premise of a sweep is that other people's orders rest there — and nobody has orders parked at a price that stopped being significant at 9:17am.

Thirteen of the twenty-five trades fired on exactly that. This is the single most likely cause of the negative gross, and it is a bug rather than a strategy failure — which means today's loss is not yet a fair test of the idea.

Not fixed today, deliberately. Changing the measuring instrument mid-measurement would have wasted the rest of the session. It goes in at tomorrow's open.
