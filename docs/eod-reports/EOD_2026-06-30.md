## 5. Findings

### Finding 1 — validating: mild-down favours longs, and the morning "flip short" call was correctly walked back
Validation 3 said mild-down days favour longs (v5 +123/trade vs shorts +11). Today confirmed it: **v5_long (all-long) was the least-bad engine, longs only −₹221**, while the short-heavy engines (S38–S46) all lost more. The shorts were green on a couple of engines (classic +₹424, cut +₹146) but couldn't offset the long damage on the concentrated books. **Flipping short-heavy at 09:48 would have deepened the loss — "hold longs, don't panic-flip" was the right read.**

### Finding 2 — complicating: a v5_flip trigger flaw, caught before launch
The morning briefly hit −0.73% (past the −0.6% hard-down trigger), so v5_flip *would* have flipped to BEAR. But NIFTY recovered to mild-down (−0.5%) and **never went green**, so v5_flip's revert condition (needs NIFTY ≥ +0.15%) would **never fire** — it would sit short-heavy all day on a longs-favoured day. **Fix needed:** revert to baseline on "back above −0.6% / above VWAP", not require full green. The A/B shadow surfaced this with zero capital at risk — and no backtest caught it, because the validations bucketed days by their *close*, hiding the intraday spike-then-recover path.

### n=1 confirmed
v5_long was the *worst* engine yesterday (06-29) and the *best* today (06-30) — opposite rankings on consecutive days. Single sessions are noise; judge on the full window.
