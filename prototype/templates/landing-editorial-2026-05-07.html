№ 03 / RISK

Behavioral risk, three layers deep.

Most algorithmic blowups don't come from a bad strategy. They come from a strategy that worked yesterday encountering a market that doesn't fit. Our risk control isn't a single stop-loss — it's a stack of three independent checks, each with a different job.

i
Corporate action skip-list
When a stock undergoes a demerger, bonus, or split, its price feed registers an artificial cliff that no algorithm can read correctly. v4 reads prototype/data/corp_actions.json at start and refuses to enter any banned name through a configurable post-event window. Saved an estimated ₹30K+ on the VEDL demerger the first day this filter was active.
5× times the corp-action filter blocked v4 from entering VEDL on a single day in the post-demerger window
ii
Per-position absolute floors
Every position carries two stops: the scorer's tactical 1% trailing-breakeven SL, plus a hard −10% of price outer floor for outlier moves. Together they handle both noise (the trailing exit) and tail risk (the absolute floor). The floor never had to fire on the fleet's best day. That's the point — it's there for the days you don't have.
−₹492 worst single-trade loss on Wednesday across 877 fleet trades
iii
Three-tier portfolio kill switch
At −₹2,500 session P&L the engine warns. At −₹5,000 it stops opening new positions but lets existing ones ride to their per-position stops. At −₹10,000 — or three positions force-closing on the −10% floor in the same day — the kill is hard: flatten everything, halt entries, close the book. Soft halt is the industry default; hard halt is reserved for a runaway.
T0 all engines closed the week at tier zero — never breached the warn line
iv
The watchlist & revival gate
When a stock exits at a loss, it's not banned — it's watched. To re-enter, four conditions must align: a Bullish Engulfing pattern on the latest 5-minute candles, volume on the trigger bar at 1.5× the 20-bar average, current price within 0.5% of the post-drop low, and time of day inside 11:15–14:45 IST (the empirically-best NSE reversal window). Two losses on the same stock in a day → blacklist it for the day.
~65% published hit rate of Bullish Engulfing + volume confirm at intraday reversals
№ 04 / HOW IT WORKS

One trading day, five movements.

The same five steps every Monday through Friday. Auto-launched at 08:45 IST, auto-closed at 15:35. The engines never sleep in.

08:45 IST
Pre-market scoring
Each engine scores the universe — 200 to 500 stocks. The dashboard archives the BUY/HOLD/SELL list at 08:42, 30 minutes before NSE opens.
09:15 IST
Initial deployment
The seven engines deploy in parallel against their independent BUY queues. Each carries its own Kelly cap, its own slot partition, its own conviction.
EVERY 10m
Scan + react
Open positions are repriced. If a stop hits, the position closes. If a target hits, the position banks. Trailing stops move on the way up.
EVERY 30m
Rescore + redeploy
A full universe rescore. New BUYs go through corp-action filter, watchlist gate, and re-entry blocker before a position is opened. Winners can re-enter freely.
15:15 — 15:35
Force exit + EOD
All open positions force-close at 15:15 — no overnight risk in Mode A. By 15:35 the auto-stop-eod fires the comparison report, the missed-trades audit, the EOD insights digest.
№ 05 / WHY THIS

You'll have read that all algos eventually fail.

You'd be right. Most do. Here's how this one is built to be different.

"If you can't tell whether a position is bleeding because the strategy is wrong or because the regime changed, you can't fix it. That's how most algorithmic systems die. Quietly. Profitably for a while. Then suddenly not."
— Engineer's note, week-1 retrospective

Most quant retail systems fail at the same place: they treat every loss the same. A −2% on a noisy day in a winning regime gets cut as fast as a −2% in the leading edge of a regime change. The first should be ignored; the second should halt every engine in the fleet.

TradePilot's design acknowledges that losses are not all the same. A position that exits on a tactical 1% stop goes to the watchlist, awaits a candle pattern, and may re-enter the same hour. A position that exits on the −10% absolute floor gets a second-strike rule. The portfolio P&L crossing −₹5,000 doesn't liquidate — it stops new entries, lets the existing positions ride to their own stops. Three different layers handle three different kinds of wrong.

That's why we run seven engines, not one. Each fails differently. The fleet's net P&L on a regime-shock day is the difference between hold-and-hope (one engine bleeds the others) and cut-watch-confirm (each engine handles its own bleeding).

The proof is on the dashboard. Not in this paragraph.
