№ 02 · Risk Control

Behavioral risk, three layers deep.

Most algorithmic blowups don't come from a bad strategy. They come from a strategy that worked yesterday encountering a market that doesn't fit. Our risk control isn't one stop-loss — it's a stack of three independent checks, each with a different job.

Layered defence

One stop-loss isn't enough. Three is.

Every position carries the scorer's tactical 1% trailing-breakeven SL plus a hard −10% absolute floor for outlier moves. The portfolio runs a three-tier kill switch on top of that. None of these have ever fired together — that's the point.

  • Corp action skip-list — saved ~₹30K from a single VEDL demerger event
  • Per-position −10% floor — caps tail risk on any single name
  • Portfolio kill switch — soft halt at −₹5K, hard kill at −₹10K
  • Watchlist & revival gate — re-entry only on engulfing + volume confirm

№ 03 · How it Works

One trading day, five movements.

The same five steps every Monday through Friday. Auto-launched at 08:45 IST, auto-closed at 15:35. The engines never sleep in.

From open to close

Engineered cadence, not gut decisions.

Every step has a precise time, a precise input, a precise output. The system never asks "should I trade now?" — it asks "what does this scan say, against this watchlist, with this kill switch tier active?"

The dashboard mirrors the same logic in real time. What you see at localhost:5050/dashboard is exactly what the engines see — same state files, same numbers, same regime context.

08:45 IST

Pre-market scoring

Each engine scores 200–500 stocks. Dashboard archives the BUY/HOLD/SELL list 30 minutes before NSE opens.

Seven engines deploy in parallel — each with its own Kelly cap, slot partition, and conviction.

Force-close at 15:15 — no overnight risk in Mode A. Reports auto-generate by 15:35.

№ 04 · Why this works
Most quant systems fail at the same place: they treat every loss the same. A 2% on a noisy day in a winning regime gets cut as fast as a 2% in the leading edge of a regime change. The first should be ignored. The second should halt every engine in the fleet.
— TRADEPILOT ENGINEERING NOTE · WEEK 1 RETROSPECTIVE

TradePilot's design acknowledges that losses are not all the same. A position that exits on a 1% tactical stop goes to the watchlist, awaits a candle pattern, and may re-enter the same hour. A position that exits on the −10% absolute floor gets a second-strike rule. The portfolio P&L crossing −₹5,000 doesn't liquidate — it stops new entries, lets existing positions ride to their own stops.

Three different layers handle three different kinds of wrong. That's why we run seven engines, not one. Each fails differently — and the fleet's net P&L on any given day is the spread between hold-and-hope (one engine bleeds the others) and cut-watch-confirm (each engine handles its own bleeding).
