Phase 02 // Risk Shield

Behavioral risk, three layers deep.

Most algorithmic blowups don't come from a bad strategy. They come from a strategy that worked yesterday meeting a market that doesn't fit. TradePilot's risk control isn't one stop-loss — it's a stack of independent checks, each with a different job.

Layered defence

One stop-loss isn't enough. Three is.

Every position carries the scorer's tactical 1% trailing-breakeven stop plus a hard −10% absolute floor for outlier moves. The portfolio runs a three-tier kill switch on top. None of these have ever fired together — that's the point.

  • Corp action skip-list — saved ~₹30K from a single VEDL demerger event
  • Per-position −10% floor — caps tail risk on any single name
  • Portfolio kill switch — soft halt at −₹5K, hard kill at −₹10K
  • Watchlist & revival gate — re-entry only on engulfing + volume confirm

> # why most quant systems fail
> Most quant systems fail at the same place: they treat every loss the same.
> A 2% loss on a noisy day in a winning regime gets cut as fast as a 2% loss
> at the leading edge of a regime change.
> The first should be ignored. The second should halt every engine in the fleet.
> — tradepilot engineering note · week 1 retrospective

TradePilot's design acknowledges that losses are not all the same. A position that exits on a 1% tactical stop goes to the watchlist, awaits a candle pattern, and may re-enter the same hour. A position that exits on the −10% absolute floor gets a second-strike rule. The portfolio P&L crossing −₹5,000 doesn't liquidate — it stops new entries and lets existing positions ride to their own stops.

Three different layers handle three different kinds of wrong. That's why the fleet runs seven engines, not one. Each fails differently — and the fleet's net P&L on any given day is the spread between hold-and-hope and cut-watch-confirm.
